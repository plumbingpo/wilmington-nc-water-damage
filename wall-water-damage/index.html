Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Wilmington

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. When water reaches the bottom of a wall, the gypsum and paper draw it upward, often a foot or more above the level the water ever reached on the floor. The painted face can look and feel normal while the back side and the insulation behind it are soaked. Looks cannot be trusted here, so every wall gets tested with a meter.

Plenty of walls that get wet can stay. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

Warning Signs

  • Fasteners and joint lines becoming visible on a once smooth wall
  • Wet tack strip, lifting planks or stained flooring at the base of the wall
  • Fuzzy or dark patches hidden in corners or in back of furniture
  • A wall that feels cool to the touch compared with others
  • Staining in a horizontal band just above the baseboard
  • Trim along the floor that has puffed up, twisted or come loose
What You Cannot See

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Walls You Cannot See

Drywall hidden in back of cabinetry and built-in units tends to be missed and dries very slowly. We take readings through toe kicks and from the opposite side to find it.

Insulation Holding Water

Batts of fiberglass absorb water and keep it pressed to the framing. A wall with wet insulation will not dry from the room side alone.

Spread Into Adjoining Rooms

The neighboring room or closet gets wet as water travels below the wall plate. We check both sides of every affected wall and the flooring along it.

Wet Sheathing Outside

Water in a perimeter wall may reach the sheathing on the far side of the cavity. Because it is slow to dry, we test it before any new drywall goes up.

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Starts Climbing

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

24 to 48 hours

Swelling and Blisters

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Days 3 through 7

Panels Lose Strength

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

After a week

Framing Is Affected

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Find Every Wet Area

Using meter readings and thermal images, our team outlines where the water went, upward and sideways, on both faces of the wall and in neighboring rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Cut or Ventilate

For walls being saved, baseboards come off and air is directed into the cavity. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on, usually 3 to 5 days, until the studs and the drywall left in place test dry. The rebuild follows with fresh insulation, drywall installation and finishing, trim and a coat of paint.
