A sump pump that quits, a sewer that backs up, a pipe that lets go, rising river water, storm surge or a hard rain can each flood a property, and the damage should be handled by professionals without delay. In nearly every case the water falls into Category 2, known as gray water, or Category 3, known as black water, which means contaminants, bacteria and other hazards that are a real health risk. For flood emergencies anywhere in Wilmington, Wilmington Restoration is available 24/7 to extract the water, disinfect affected areas and bring the property back safely.

The first job is getting the water out quickly, and our submersible and truck-mounted pumps are able to clear thousands of gallons every hour. With the standing water gone, our IICRC-certified technicians check the contamination level and apply the right sanitizing steps, including antimicrobial treatment, HEPA air filtration and disinfection of every affected surface. Materials beyond saving, like contaminated drywall, damaged insulation and saturated carpet pad, are removed and disposed of the right way.

Then the drying begins: high-velocity air movers and commercial dehumidifiers of the LGR (low-grain refrigerant) type go in to pull moisture out of the building's structural parts. Daily readings from calibrated moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera tell us when everything is fully dry, which is what keeps mold from growing. Your insurance adjuster will want scope-of-work reports, equipment logs, moisture maps and photographs, so we keep that documentation going through the whole project.

When to Call for Flood Cleanup in Wilmington

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Any amount of water sitting in a room, garage or basement
  • Appliances such as a water heater, washer or dishwasher spilling water across the floor
  • A layer of silt, dirt or debris on the floor after water recedes
  • Water coming in through foundation cracks, window wells or door thresholds
  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or drywall that feels soft and wet
  • Stains along baseboards and lower walls that mark the flood level
  • Heavy rainfall, a dead sump pump and water spreading across the basement

Why Professional Flood Cleanup Matters

Flood cleanup is far more than pumping out standing water. Chemicals, bacteria, sewage and other contamination ride in with the flood and create real health dangers. A trained cleanup team knows how to set containment, wear PPE (personal protective equipment) correctly and sanitize by protocol, which protects your household from hepatitis, E. coli and other diseases carried in water.

Health is not the only issue: when cleanup is done wrong, moisture stays hidden and heavy mold can follow in a matter of days. With thermal imaging, psychrometric calculations and moisture meters, trained technicians confirm that subfloors, structural framing, wall cavities and every other wet area dry to an acceptable standard. This scientific approach to drying is the only reliable way to prevent secondary damage that can cost thousands more to fix.

Maintenance Tips for Flood Cleanup

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Consider flood insurance even if you are not in a designated flood zone
  • Keep documents and keepsakes in watertight bins on shelves, not on a basement floor
  • Add a sump pump with battery backup, since power outages and storms tend to arrive together
  • Add downspout extensions so roof water empties well away from the foundation
  • Seal foundation cracks and apply a waterproofing coating to basement walls
  • In areas of the property that tend to flood, raise electrical systems and appliances off the floor
