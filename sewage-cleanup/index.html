A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. Wilmington Restoration provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Wilmington, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

When to Call for Sewage Cleanup in Wilmington

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Multiple drains in your home backing up at the same time
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains when other fixtures are used
  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line
  • Family members falling ill, especially with gastrointestinal trouble, for no obvious reason
  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs
  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Fit a strainer over each sink and shower drain so hair and debris stay out of the pipes
  • A drain that is slowing down should be dealt with early, while it is still partly open
  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it
  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs

Why Sewage Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.
