Why Professional Storm Damage Repair Matters

Storm damage creates complex, connected repair needs that take experienced restoration professionals to handle properly. What seems like a small leak indoors can hide widespread water damage in wall cavities, roof decking and insulation, which turns into mold and structural decay unless it is identified and dried properly. Our technicians trace where the water traveled using thermal imaging plus moisture meters, so no affected area is overlooked.

Emergency board-up and tarping also take proper technique to be effective. If a tarp is fastened poorly, it can direct water where you do not want it or give out when the wind picks up. Storm Damage Repair Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • In areas that see hurricanes, fit storm shutters or windows rated for impact
  • Know how to shut off your main water supply in case a storm damages a pipe
  • Trim trees near your home to lower the risk of wind-driven branch damage
  • For insurance purposes, keep a yearly set of pictures showing the state of your property
  • Clean out downspouts and gutters regularly, which lets heavy rainfall drain away as it should
  • Runoff should flow away from your foundation, so fix low spots and add downspout extensions

Warning Signs for Storm Damage Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Shingles that are gone, cracked or curled up when you look from the yard
  • Damaged or displaced flashing around roof penetrations, valleys or edges
  • A musty smell indoors in the days after heavy rain
  • New brown rings or damp marks on walls and ceilings once the storm passes
  • Wet insulation in the attic or wall cavities found after bad weather
  • Puddles near skylights, beside a chimney or on the attic floor
  • Sagging or detached gutters that spill rainwater down the siding
