Restoration Challenges Winston-Salem Homeowners Face

Not every water problem announces itself with a flooded floor. Below are the situations our crews are called to most often around Winston-Salem, and what each one tends to involve.

Hidden leaks: A pinhole in a supply line or a loose drain fitting can drip inside a wall for months. Watch for a water bill that climbs for no reason, soft spots on the floor, and paint that blisters. Our technicians trace the wet area with moisture meters and thermal cameras, without tearing out more wall than needed. Left alone, constant dampness rots framing and feeds mold, so a minor drip is still worth fixing now.

Sewage: A blocked sewer line sends waste back into the house, usually at the lowest shower, toilet or floor drain. It is typically caused by roots in the line, built-up grease or a municipal sewer overwhelmed during a downpour. Sewage counts as Category 3, the most contaminated class, and it is not safe to clean up bare-handed. Porous items it touches, such as carpet and pad, generally have to be removed.

Storms: Hard rain can outrun gutters and yard drains, then pool against the house. From there it comes in under doors, through window wells and across garage floors. Floodwater from outside picks up soil, fertilizer and whatever else it crossed, so we treat it as contaminated. Homes across Winston-Salem can see this in any season with a strong enough storm.

Crawlspace: In homes built over a crawl space, few people look underneath, so moisture there builds up unseen. Standing water, a torn vapor barrier or a leaking pipe overhead keeps the air below your floors humid. Over time that leads to sagging insulation, musty odors indoors and soft or cupped flooring. Our team removes the water, dries the framing and can point out what is letting moisture in.

Condensate: An air conditioner pulls gallons of water out of the air on a humid day. That water should leave through a small drain line, which clogs easily with algae and dust. A blockage makes the drip pan spill over, wetting the ceiling or floor near the indoor unit. In Winston-Salem we often find this only after a stain or sagging drywall gives it away, so a yearly drain check helps.
