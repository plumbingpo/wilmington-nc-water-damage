Wet Carpet Drying: What Wilmington Homeowners Should Know

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. A shop vacuum and a stack of towels only deal with the surface. Our team in Wilmington extracts the water, checks every layer with moisture meters, and sets up drying that reaches all the way down.

Saving a carpet comes down to how long it stayed wet and whether the water that soaked it was clean. If the water came out of a clean supply line and we reach it fast, the carpet can normally stay down while it dries. Gray water from an appliance often means the pad goes and the carpet gets cleaned and treated. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

Signs to Look For

  • Brown or yellow stains wicking up to the tips of the fibers as it dries
  • Carpet that still feels cold and clammy to bare feet days after the spill
  • Ripples or loose spots where the carpet has let go of the tack strip
  • Soft or squeaky subfloor beneath the area of carpet that was soaked
  • A squishy feel under your feet, or wet socks after walking across the room
  • Baseboards that have swollen or paint that bubbles low on the wall
Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Mold Under the Carpet

The dark, damp underside of the carpet is where mold usually begins. You smell it long before you ever see it on top.

Wet Wall Bottoms

The base of each wall is where water moving beneath the carpet ends up. Drywall and baseboards wick it upward, and nobody notices because the carpet covers the edge.

Water Quality Drops

Even clean water goes bad the longer it sits. As it sits in carpet and pad it picks up soil and bacteria, so a carpet that could have been dried may have to be removed.

Water Held in the Pad

Water many times heavier than the pad itself can sit inside it. Long after the top feels dry, that pad is still passing moisture up into the carpet and down into the floor.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Spreads Sideways

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

Hours 24 to 48

Smell and Mold Start

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

After a week

Past Saving

At this stage, removing both carpet and pad is usually the only option. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Edges

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Extract the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry All the Layers

Some jobs call for floating the carpet on a cushion of air, others for lifting it and taking the pad out. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until carpet and subfloor both read dry.

4

Clean and Reset

We treat the carpet and subfloor with an antimicrobial where needed, lay new pad if the old one came out, restretch the carpet onto the tack strip, and clean it.
