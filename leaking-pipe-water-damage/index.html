Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Wilmington: What to Know

You seldom catch a pipe leak the day it begins. It drips inside a wall, under a sink or below the slab, and the first thing you notice is a stain, a soft spot in the floor or a water bill that makes no sense. By then the materials around the pipe have often been wet for weeks. Our team in Wilmington finds how far the moisture has spread and dries all of it, not just the part you can see.

A plumber can stop the drip, though the water that soaked into the structure is still there afterward. This is where our team comes in. With meters and a thermal camera we locate every wet material, make only the openings required, then keep drying equipment running until moisture levels read normal again. A slow leak gives mold plenty of time to begin, so we inspect for growth too and treat any surface it has reached.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off the WaterClose the fixture's supply valve if the leak is at a sink or toilet. When the source is unclear, the house main is the valve to turn off.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityTurn off the breaker serving the area when water is close to a light fixture, switch or outlet. Leave any wet cord or electrical device alone.
  3. Check the Water MeterShut off all taps and fixtures, then go look at the water meter. Movement on the dial means water is escaping out of sight, perhaps beneath the slab.
  4. Clear Out and Take PhotosClear out the cabinet or closet near the leak and take photos of stains, warped materials and the pipe itself before anything is repaired or thrown away.
  5. Get a Moisture CheckAsk for meter readings of the area before anyone patches it closed. A wall that feels dry on the surface can still be wet inside the cavity.
Coverage

Insurance and Leaking Pipe Water Damage

A slow leak and a sudden one are not handled the same way by insurance. Coverage is common for accidental, sudden damage, while a leak that ran for months or weeks is usually not covered and is specifically excluded in some policies. An adjuster may look more kindly on a leak hidden from view than on one that could be seen and was left alone. Read your policy and talk to your agent. We document the source of the leak, moisture readings and photos so the adjuster has clear facts.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • Tiny pinholes that form as corrosion gradually works through aging copper pipe.
  • High water pressure that stresses joints and valves year after year until one weeps.
  • Drain fittings and P-traps below sinks that work loose and leak whenever water goes down.
  • Fasteners from remodeling work or wall hangings that nick or pierce a line inside the wall.
  • Soil movement and rubbing that wear a hole in a line buried below the slab.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Hidden Mold Growth

Nothing suits mold better than the dark, moist space inside a wall. It can spread across the hidden face of the drywall long before anything appears on the room side, which is common when a leak is slow.

Swollen Cabinets

Sink cabinet bottoms are usually particleboard, which swells and crumbles when it soaks up a leak. They do not return to shape, so long leaks often mean replacing the base.

Decayed Wall Framing

Studs and the plate beneath them soften and decay when they sit wet for weeks. Wood that has lost its strength has to be cut out and replaced.

Flooring Damage

A leak in a wall or under the slab sends water beneath the floor covering. Hardwood cups, laminate swells at the seams, and adhesive under vinyl and tile lets go.
