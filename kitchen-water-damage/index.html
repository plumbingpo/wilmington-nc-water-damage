Kitchen Water Damage Help in Wilmington

No room has as many water connections as the kitchen, with its faucet, drain, disposal, dishwasher and refrigerator line. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. When one drips, the water runs to the back of the cabinet base and spreads under the floor. For homeowners in Wilmington, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Particleboard boxes swell fast and stay swollen, but solid wood and plywood frequently dry well. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

Signs to Look For

  • Sticking cabinet doors, or doors whose edges no longer match up
  • A damp, stale smell when you open the cabinet under the sink
  • Soft or discolored vinyl flooring near the refrigerator or along the cabinet line
  • Paint that bubbles, or a stain, on the wall at the back of the sink or dishwasher
  • A water bill that climbs, or a faint hiss or drip sound, with every tap turned off
  • Swelling and flaking where the cabinet sides meet the floor
Our Process

Our Kitchen Water Damage Process

1

Stop the Source

We shut off the leaking fixture or appliance line, pull the dishwasher or refrigerator if needed, and confirm exactly which connection failed so it can be repaired before drying begins.

2

Map the Moisture

We check the cabinet bases, the wall at their back and the floor throughout the room with thermal imaging and moisture meters. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry Without Demolition

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

We carefully remove any flooring, subfloor or cabinet section that cannot be saved. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Drips at the Sink Drain

Disposal seals and drain fittings that are loose leak only when water is going down, which makes them easy to overlook. A damp sink cabinet smells and grows mold quickly because that water has food waste in it.

Toe Kick Space

The gap under base cabinets is closed on every side. Water pools in it for weeks, and the mold that grows goes unseen until the cabinets come out.

The Ice Maker Line

A few drops per minute can escape from the narrow tube that supplies the ice maker. When the fridge never gets moved, this amount will rot the flooring and the wall.

Layers of Flooring

Kitchens often have new flooring laid over old. Trapped water sits between the two, with no way to evaporate and no sign on the surface.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First hour

Water Goes Underneath

A failed fitting or hose sends water under appliances and to the rear of the cabinets. Seams and edges let it get below the flooring.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Start Swelling

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. Behind the dishwasher and beneath the cabinets, the closed, dark space allows mold to begin.

Day 3 to 7

Floors Begin Moving

Hardwood cups, laminate edges puff up, and vinyl starts to lift as its adhesive lets go. Drawers and cabinet doors stop lining up.

After a week

Tear Out Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. Mold at the back of the cabinets often forces their removal, countertop included, and the subfloor may be delaminating.
