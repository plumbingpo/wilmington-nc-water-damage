Water Damage to Hardwood Floors in Wilmington, NC

Home / Blog / Hardwood Floor Water Damage Repair in Wilmington, NC

A hardwood floor adds real value to a home, yet water can damage it more readily than almost any other feature. Cupping, crowning, buckling, staining, and mold in the subfloor beneath are all results of water reaching hardwood. If you own hardwood in Wilmington, NC, you should know what to do when it gets wet, and how to tell floors a professional can save from floors that must be replaced.

How Water Damages Hardwood

The term for wood is hygroscopic: it gains or loses moisture depending on its surroundings. Wood fibers expand as they take on moisture, which is what happens when a floor gets too wet. The earliest sign you can see of a moisture imbalance is cupping, where board edges sit higher than the middle. If moisture exposure continues, boards may crown (center rising higher than edges as the top dries faster than the bottom) or buckle (lifting entirely off the subfloor). Mold is another risk of long exposure, since it grows unseen between the subfloor and the hardwood and may not be found until the floor comes up.

Is a Wet Hardwood Floor Salvageable?

Saving a wet hardwood floor comes down to the subfloor's condition, the hardwood's type and finish, the length of time it stayed wet, the amount of water, and how soon professional drying starts. In many cases, hardwood floors that receive professional drying within 24 to 48 hours of clean water exposure can be saved. What makes it work is slow, controlled drying, so moisture leaves the wood evenly and the boards do not split or crack.

Drying Wood Floors the Professional Way

Wood floors need a specialized approach that differs from the standard methods used on the rest of a structure. Technicians lay panels or drying mats on top of the floor, which sets up controlled conditions and draws moisture out of the boards evenly. Floor drying systems can also be used beneath the subfloor from crawl spaces. Everything depends on an even, gradual pace, since wood dried too fast loses surface moisture before the core does and ends up permanently cupped, cracked, and split.

Signs the Floor Cannot Be Saved

Replacement is usually the answer if the subfloor is compromised or has mold on it, the boards have buckled or delaminated extensively, sewage, floodwater, or other contaminated water touched the wood, or the floor stayed wet longer than 48 to 72 hours.
