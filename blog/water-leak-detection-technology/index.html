Leak Detection Systems Compared

Leak detection can be as simple as a standalone sensor or as comprehensive as a whole-home system. A point sensor is a small battery-powered unit that sits near a sink, washing machine, water heater, or other risk area and sounds an alarm if it detects water. Smart sensors connect to your WiFi network and send smartphone alerts when moisture is detected, even when you're away from home. A whole-home system watches the main water line for unusual flow and can shut the water off automatically once it detects a leak.

Where to Place Leak Detectors

To get the most from your sensors, put one beside the dishwasher, by the water heater, below each bathroom sink and the kitchen sink, at the back of the washing machine, next to any refrigerator with a water line, close to the sump pump or elsewhere in the basement, along HVAC condensate drains, and in any crawl space or utility area that has plumbing.

Professional Leak Detection

When leaks are suspected but not visible, professional leak detection services use advanced technology to locate hidden water intrusion. Thermal imaging cameras reveal temperature differences that indicate moisture behind walls and under floors. Water flowing in a hidden pipe makes a sound, and acoustic devices are built to hear it. For precise moisture content numbers in building materials, technicians rely on moisture meters.
