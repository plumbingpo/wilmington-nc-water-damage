Dealing With Roof Leak Water Damage in Wilmington

Where you see the stain and where the rain gets in are usually two different places. Water enters at a raised shingle, a split in the flashing or a worn skylight seal, follows the rafters and decking downhill, and finally lands on the insulation. Often the attic has been damp for some time before any brown mark reaches the room below. Our team maps everything that got wet, then dries it.

To be clear, roof repair is not what we do, water damage is. We work on the interior, which means removing wet insulation, drying drywall and framing, dealing with mold and rebuilding the ceiling. When the roof is still letting water through, a temporary tarp is something we can install, and we will coordinate with your roofer so the repair comes before the ceiling is closed.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsIf water is dripping through a light fixture, a ceiling fan or near an outlet, switch off that circuit at the breaker and leave the fixture alone.
  2. Catch the DripsFurniture, electronics and rugs should be moved aside, with buckets placed wherever water is falling. Protect the floor with plastic sheeting or old towels.
  3. Drain a Bulging CeilingWhen water pools in a bubble overhead, pierce the low point with a screwdriver while standing clear, and let it run into a bucket.
  4. Stay Off the RoofClimbing on slick shingles or crawling around a dark attic is not worth the risk. Note from inside where the water is coming in and leave the rest to professionals.
  5. Photograph the DamageUse your phone to record the damaged ceiling, attic and belongings first, hold off on throwing things out until that is done, and call us to get the drying started.
Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

When a leak happens suddenly and by accident, for example a branch going through the roof or wind stripping shingles, many homeowners policies pay for the water damage inside. Leaks blamed on age, wear or skipped maintenance are usually denied, and the roof repair itself may be handled differently from the interior. Our team gives your adjuster photos and moisture readings, yet what is covered comes down to your policy, so check it or talk to your agent.

Our Process

Our Roof Leak Water Damage Process

1

Map Where Water Went

We inspect the attic, ceilings and wall cavities with moisture meters and thermal imaging to map everywhere the rain traveled, including areas that still look dry from the room below.

2

Cover and Protect

When rain can still get in, a temporary tarp from us or a call to your roofer comes first. Floors and belongings below the wet ceiling are covered as well.

Unsalvageable drywall and insulation come out, and dehumidifiers and air movers go into the attic space and each room under it, with readings taken until the framing has dried.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Water backing up at the eave and under the shingles because the gutters are blocked.
  • Holes and fractures left in the roof after a branch or hailstones hit it.
  • Shingles that are cracked, curled or blown off by wind, exposing the underlayment.
  • Sun-cracked rubber collars where plumbing vent pipes come through the roof.
  • A skylight whose curb or seal has dried and cracked, so rain gets past it.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Rafters and Decking

Rafters, trusses and decking that get wet again and again begin to darken, swell and soften. Rot in these members weakens the structure that holds the roof up.

Ceiling Fixtures and Wiring

Recessed cans, ceiling fans and electrical boxes are low points where roof water collects and drips through. Wet connections can short or corrode, so the circuit should stay off until it is checked.

Inside the Walls

From the attic, a leak can drop into a stud bay and soak the wall insulation and drywall from behind. Often the one clue is paint that blisters or trim that swells at the floor.

Attic Insulation

Wet insulation mats down, stops insulating and holds water against the ceiling drywall for days. In some cases fiberglass batts dry out and stay. Removal is the usual answer for wet cellulose.
