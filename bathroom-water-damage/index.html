Bathroom Water Damage Help in Wilmington

Bathrooms are built to get wet on the surface, not underneath it. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. Hidden from view, it builds up behind the wall tile and down in the subfloor. Our Wilmington team finds where the water is getting in, measures how far it has gone, and dries or removes what is wet.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. With thermal imaging and moisture meters, we map what is wet before any demolition, and we open just the sections that will not dry in place.

What It Looks Like

  • A toilet that rocks slightly, or dark staining around its base
  • On the wall beside the shower, baseboard that has swelled or paint that is peeling
  • Edges of the vinyl floor peeling up, or dark discoloration showing through it
  • A brown ring or sagging spot on the ceiling directly below the bathroom
  • The caulk line at the shower or tub has split, pulled away, or turned black
  • Soft, springy flooring alongside the tub or where you stand at the vanity
Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Damage Downstairs

Water follows pipes and joists before it drips, so the stain downstairs may sit several feet from the actual leak. Wiring and light fixtures may lie along that route.

Rot Under the Toilet

A failed wax ring leaks a little with every flush. The toilet eventually rocks because this category 3 water has rotted the subfloor at the flange.

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Supply valves and drain fittings under the sink can drip for months behind stored bottles and towels. The cabinet floor swells, and the water runs through to the subfloor and the wall behind it.

Trapped Steam

Steam lingers after each shower when the exhaust fan is weak or absent. Mold feeds on the moisture that lands on ceilings and walls, and damp materials dry far more slowly.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first few days

Water Slips Through

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

First few weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

The wood under the tile never gets a chance to dry between showers. Mold gets going in the wall cavity and on the floor's underside.

One to three months

Signs Show Up

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. If there is a room below, a stain appears on its ceiling and slowly widens.

Six months or more

The Structure Suffers

Subfloor around the toilet or tub turns soft and joists may begin to rot. Drying alone no longer fixes it, because carpentry and new tile are now part of the repair.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Bathroom Water Damage

1

Find the Leak

We test the tub, shower, toilet and supply lines one at a time, and use moisture meters and thermal imaging to see where water has traveled under tile and inside walls.

2

Open What Is Needed

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Cavities

The subfloor, the framing in the walls and the space above the lower ceiling are dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Seal

We replace subfloor and backer board, and can coordinate tile, a new toilet seal, fresh caulk and ceiling repair so the leak path is closed, not covered over.
