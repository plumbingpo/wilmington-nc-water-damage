How to Prevent Mold Remediation Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly mold issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Do not leave damp laundry, bath mats or spilled water sitting for long
  • Laundry rooms, baths and kitchens all need working exhaust ventilation that is sized for the space
  • Mold-resistant paint and wallboard are the better choice wherever moisture is common, such as a basement or bath
  • Aim for 30 to 50% indoor humidity and never let it stay above 60%, running dehumidifiers and ventilating as needed
  • Deal quickly with condensation that forms on pipes, cold surfaces or window glass
  • Keep gutters clean so rainwater does not seep in along the foundation

The Benefits of Professional Mold Remediation

This is not a job to take on yourself. Without containment, scrubbing or tearing out moldy material releases spores by the millions, potentially spreading contamination through the house and making the health exposure worse than it was. Capturing and containing the spores during removal takes HEPA-filtered scrubbers, protective equipment for the crew and containment kept under negative air pressure, which is how professionals do it.

Just as important, we deal with moisture, the underlying cause of every mold problem. Removing visible mold without correcting the underlying moisture problem means the mold will return, often within weeks. Certified remediation technicians have the training and equipment to identify hidden moisture sources, dry affected areas properly and put solutions in place that prevent recurrence.

When to Call for Mold Remediation in Wilmington

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A slow leak under a sink, behind an appliance or in the roof that went unnoticed
  • An earthy, musty smell that will not go away, mostly in humid or closed-off spaces
  • Recent water damage or flooding that was not professionally dried
  • Windows, cold surfaces or walls that sweat with condensation on a regular basis
  • Cleaning that never lasts, with growth returning to the same surface
  • Fuzzy or spotted growth on stored boxes, clothing or the backs of furniture
  • Visible mold growth of any color on walls, ceilings, flooring or other surfaces
